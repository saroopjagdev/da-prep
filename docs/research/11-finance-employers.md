## What this means (JUDGEMENT)

1. **Biggest content gap: nine finance employers with a real Level 6 programme have no profile**: Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, Deutsche Bank, UBS, Citi, BNY, Rothschild & Co, CIBC, Bank of England (plus the FCA). These are exactly the "high finance" names our users search for. Bank of America, Deutsche Bank, UBS and Morgan Stanley matter most.
2. **Several famous names do not run degree apprenticeships** (Lazard, BlackRock, Schroders, BNP Paribas, likely Nomura, Fidelity, M&G, L&G). Telling students this clearly is useful and honest. A "Who actually offers a finance degree apprenticeship?" page would also be strong for SEO.
3. **The 2027 season has already started.** Goldman Sachs, Barclays, Deloitte, KPMG and EY are open now (early October 2026). J.P. Morgan opened in early September. The app's dates for Barclays (Oct-Nov), Lloyds (3 Nov) and Goldman (Operations missing) need updating first.
4. **Applied Finance at Queen Mary is the common front-office degree** (GS FICC, Deutsche IBCM, BofA Global Markets as "Applied Finance"). Exeter is the other big partner (UBS, Rothschild, HSBC, DB Tech). A short explainer on these degrees would help students compare offers.
5. **Pay for finance DAs clusters at about £24k-£32k** (BoE £25,270; Barclays £25,200; Santander £27,500; Lloyds from £26,500, Risk London £31,700; JPM Scotland £24,000). Investment-bank salaries are mostly unpublished.

## Day-to-day roles in plain English (JUDGEMENT; for the app's "what you'd actually do" sections)

| Role / division | What you'd actually do at 18-19 |
|---|---|
| Global Markets / Sales & Trading (FICC, Equities) | Sit with traders and salespeople. Update pricing spreadsheets, book and check trades, prepare the morning market summary, answer simple client data requests. FICC = bonds, currencies, commodities; Equities = shares |
| Investment Banking / IBCM / Advisory | Help bankers who advise companies on raising money (bonds = DCM, shares = ECM) or on buying/selling businesses (M&A). Research companies, build spreadsheets of their numbers, make presentation slides ("pitch books") |
| Operations | Make sure every trade actually happens: money and securities arrive in the right place on time. Spot and fix "breaks" (mismatches), talk to other banks' back offices |
| COO (Chief Operating Office) | Help run the business: track revenue and costs, controls, projects and reports for senior managers |
| Technology / Engineering | Write, test and support the software used by traders, operations and clients. Rotate through teams to cover degree modules |
| Data Science | Clean and analyse data, build dashboards and simple models for the desk |
| Risk / Compliance | Check the bank isn't taking more risk than allowed and follows the rules; prepare reports, review trades and client files |
| Audit (Big 4 and internal audit) | Check whether a company's accounts and controls are right: test samples of transactions, talk to clients, document findings; study for ACA/ACCA |
| Corporate / Commercial Banking | Support relationship managers who look after business clients: prepare credit (lending) papers, analyse company accounts, client meetings |
| Wealth Management | Support advisers who manage money for wealthy individuals: client reports, portfolio data, meeting preparation |
| Central bank / regulator (BoE, FCA) | Support teams that set interest rates, supervise banks or protect consumers; in tech routes, build their systems |
